---
title: What is a transient grating, in one picture?
date: 2026-10-19
summary: Two pulses, one interference pattern, and a stopwatch for nanoscale motion.
tags: [explainer, physics]
---

Cross two short laser pulses inside a sample and they interfere. Where the waves add up, the material absorbs more energy; where they cancel, it absorbs less. The result is a **grating**: a periodic pattern of heat, strain or magnetisation, with a period set by the wavelength and the crossing angle.

That pattern does not sit still. It launches sound waves, it diffuses away as heat, and in magnetic materials it rings as spin waves. A third pulse, arriving a little later, diffracts off whatever is left. Scan the delay and you get a movie of how the pattern evolves.

With visible light the smallest period is a few hundred nanometres. With extreme-ultraviolet and X-ray pulses from free-electron lasers we can go down to a few nanometres, which is where things get interesting: continuum models of heat and sound start to break down.
