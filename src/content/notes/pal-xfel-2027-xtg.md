**When and where:** May 2027, PAL-XFEL, the free-electron laser of the Pohang Accelerator Laboratory in South Korea.

This beamtime has two goals that feed each other: building an instrument and doing science with it.

### A dedicated XTG chamber

X-ray transient grating (XTG) experiments need two X-ray beams crossing on the sample at a small, well-controlled angle, with stable overlap in space and time. On top of that, the setup has to pick up a weak diffracted beam next to much brighter ones.

At most facilities this geometry is improvised on general-purpose end stations. We are building a chamber designed for it from the start. It holds the optics that split and recombine the FEL beam, the sample environment, and a detector positioned to collect the grating signal.

The aim is a setup that can be aligned quickly and reproduces the same geometry from one beamtime to the next. That would make XTG a standard tool at PAL-XFEL rather than a one-off.

### Self-diffraction XTG at the oxygen edge

We will commission the chamber with a self-diffraction experiment. The two crossed pulses write an interference grating into the sample, and the same pulses diffract from the grating they create. No separate probe is needed, which keeps the first experiment simple. The diffracted intensity then reports how strongly and how quickly the material responds while the pulse is still passing through.

Tuning the photon energy to the **oxygen K edge** (around 530 eV) makes the grating element-specific. The signal comes from excitations localised on oxygen atoms, which is where much of the interesting physics happens in oxides, water and many molecular systems. By scanning the photon energy across the edge and changing the pulse intensity, we can separate:

- resonant, element-specific contributions from the non-resonant background;
- the linear response from the nonlinear onset that makes four-wave mixing with X-rays possible.

If it works, the result will be a new instrument, a first set of oxygen-edge XTG data, and the groundwork for pump–probe XTG experiments at PAL-XFEL in the following years.
